THE THREAD RUNNING THROUGH ALL OF IT
  Collecting action demonstrations for every robot does not scale; rendering a robot
  from its URDF does. Can static embodiment data -- never used for policy learning --
  let a policy act on robots it has no demonstrations for? Everything below is an
  attempt to answer some part of that.

PART 1 — THE CONTRASTIVE VISUAL ENCODERS (what you asked about first)
================================================================================

1.1 WHAT THEY ARE
    SigLIP vision towers (86.4M params, from SmolVLM2-500M) pre-trained on
    eef_pairs renders, where 56 embodiments are posed at the SAME canonical EEF
    states. Positive pair = same pose, different robot. Trained with supervised
    contrastive loss, and later with EEF-state and EEF-pixel regression heads.

    LOCATION  outputs/siglip_pretrain/<tag>/
              vision_tower.safetensors     the tower
              pretrain_info.json           args AND the held-out embodiment list
              head_eef_pixel.pt            the pixel head, where trained
              head_eef_state.pt            the state head, where trained

    THE TOWERS
      stock                          untrained reference (no pre-training)
      contrastive_mean_b16           contrastive only, mean pooling
      contrastive_tokens_b16         contrastive only, per-token
      eef_mean_b16 / eef_attn_b16    EEF-state regression only
      both_*_b16 (4 variants)        contrastive + EEF, mean/token x 4/6 views
      all4_n4 / n8 / n16 / n32 / n42 the embodiment-count ladder (§1.3)
      all4_n42_eefstate              EEF state only, 42 embodiments
      all4_n42_eefpixel              EEF pixel only, 42 embodiments
      all4_n42_all                   contrastive + state + pixel  <- THE ONE TO USE

1.2 WHAT THEY WERE TESTED ON — held-out embodiment evaluation
    tools/eval_heldout_embodiment.py, results in outputs/heldout_*.json
    Three metrics, because the cosine gap alone cannot separate invariance from
    collapse: A/B/C pair split, pose retrieval top-1 (query an UNSEEN embodiment
    against seen ones), and a linear embodiment probe (should fall to chance).

    RESULT: the representation transfers, and had not saturated at 42 embodiments.
      embodiments  held-out gap  pose retr@1  emb-probe  (held-out size)
      stock            -0.099      0.118-0.210   0.15-0.52     --
      4                +0.329      0.587         0.024         52
      8                +0.646      0.785         0.000         48
      16               +0.668      0.848         0.005         40
      32               +0.973      0.969         0.009         24
      42               +0.994      0.987         0.015         14
    CAVEAT: the held-out set SHRINKS as N grows (52 -> 14), so the x axis moves the
    test set too. Stated on the figure rather than hidden.
    FIGURES outputs/heldout_scaling.png, outputs/heldout_objectives.png

    OBJECTIVES at 42 embodiments, same 14 held out:
      contrastive      gap +0.994  retr 0.987  probe 0.015
      EEF state        gap +0.798  retr 0.969  probe 0.029
      EEF pixel        gap +0.154  retr 0.799  probe 0.324   <- barely invariant
      all three        gap +0.988  retr 0.982  probe 0.000   <- use this one

1.3 WHERE THE TOWERS WERE THEN USED — and the result that reframed everything
    outputs/dp_embodiment/{scratch,frozen,finetune,online}/
      A language-conditioned Diffusion Policy on PnPSinkToCounter (IIWA/Panda/UR5e),
      30k steps, comparing a pre-trained tower frozen / fine-tuned / trained online
      against a stock tower.
      RESULT, step-matched:  scratch 0.0387  frozen 0.0388  finetune 0.0383
                             online 0.0395
      All within 0.001. The pre-trained tower bought the policy NOTHING.

    THE DIAGNOSIS (tools/predict_eef_pixel.py, measure_feature_displacement.py):
      The EEF-pixel head locates the gripper to 3-5 px on embodiments it never saw
      -- but collapses to near chance on real policy images. Perturbing an image and
      measuring how far its feature moves, as a fraction of a real pose change:
          different background    1%     shift 40 px          31%
          furniture recolour      2%     zoom out 0.75x       41%
          A DIFFERENT ROBOT       4%     a policy-corpus frame 102%
      The invariance that got trained is to embodiment and appearance. It is NOT
      invariance to camera geometry, and a policy frame is fully decorrelated.
      That is why a frozen contrastive tower matched a frozen stock one.
    FIGURES outputs/eef_pixel_*.png, outputs/feature_displacement.png

PART 2 — CROSS-EMBODIMENT MOTION PREDICTION (two images -> Cartesian delta)
================================================================================

2.1 EXPERIMENTS 1 & 2 — synthetic, MLP head on the tower
    outputs/motion_prediction/{exp1_n42, exp2A_n*, exp2B_n*}/
      results.json (per-embodiment breakdown), motion_head.pt, vision_tower.safetensors

    Q1 does motion understanding generalise to unseen morphology?  YES
      42 train / 14 held-out embodiments:
        seen      0.41 cm   dir cos 0.993   rot  8.2 deg   grip 0.992
        HELD-OUT  0.67 cm   dir cos 0.980   rot 10.8 deg   grip 0.970
        baselines: predict-the-mean 4.66 cm, identity rotation 60.2 deg

    Q2 does embodiment diversity help, or is it just more data?  DIVERSITY
      protocol A grows the data with embodiment count, B holds it fixed at 60k:
        n     A samples  A MAE    B samples  B MAE
        2       8,000    3.61 cm    60,000   3.56 cm
        4      16,000    2.54       60,000   2.34
        8      32,000    1.73       60,000   1.79
        16     64,000    1.20       60,000   1.16
        42    168,000    0.74       60,000   0.84
      A ends with 2.8x B's data and the curves are indistinguishable.
      CONTROL: seen-split error moves the OTHER way, 0.27 -> 0.46 cm. Few
      embodiments is memorisation, not an easier problem.
    FIGURE outputs/motion_experiments.png

2.2 POSE-DIVERSITY LADDER — and a confound worth remembering
    outputs/motion_prediction/{pose48,pose96,pose144}/, outputs/pose_ladder_common.json
      42 embodiments and 60k samples held FIXED, only pose count varies.
        poses  scored on its OWN pose set   scored on ONE COMMON pool
        48            0.60 cm                      2.18 cm
        96            0.74                         1.13
        144           0.84                         0.83
      Read raw, it says more poses is WORSE. That is task difficulty, not model
      quality: each run was tested on pairs from its own pose subset. On one common
      pool the ordering reverses and pose diversity is worth 2.6x.
      GENERAL LESSON: any ladder that varies the data also varies the test set
      unless you force it not to.
    FIGURE outputs/pose_ladder.png

2.3 EXPERIMENT 3 — does the motion objective help a POLICY?  CANNOT TELL

      RESOLUTION IS THE ONE BIG EFFECT: prediction diversity 30-49% -> 82% at
      512x910, and rotation 0.147-0.168 -> 0.200-0.212. Predicted: at 180x320 a
      5-degree rotation moves the gripper's extremities 0.87 px, a ninth of a patch.

      UNSEEN HARDWARE IS NEARLY FREE: seen minus held-out is +0.006 to +0.031 with
      seed noise 0.010, and the `arm` split -- arms never seen in any combination --
      has the SMALLEST gap. Note that a RANDOM holdout on this 8x6 grid leaves every
      arm and gripper in training, so it tests unseen COMBINATIONS, not hardware.

      BUT NOTHING IS GOOD ANYWHERE. Even in the densest 30 px of the frame, on
      trained embodiments, the best model reaches 0.195. Both splits plateau
      together, so the bottleneck is neither morphology nor coverage.
      Interpretable breakdown (`high`, 400 samples): exact sentence 0.000 in 400
      attempts; direction recall 0.600 but the model asserts motion on a stationary
      axis 79-91% of the time, so that is coverage rather than discrimination; and
      the magnitude word is a constant "slightly" (0.045 correct on "moderately",
      0.000 on "far").

      THE REAL-DATA COLUMN IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY: models trained on camera-frame labels
      are scored against END-EFFECTOR-frame labels, a convention none of them saw.
      My error. Comparisons within the camera-frame runs still hold.

DESIGN POINTS THAT MATTER WHEN READING THE EVAL
  * The action corpus AND the policy config are identical in all eight. Only the
    auxiliary differs. B vs C is the hinge: same question, same answer format, only
    the frames' embodiments differ, so any gap is embodiment diversity.
  * Answers use observation.eef_base_rel, NOT eef_pose. libero_object seats the base
    on the floor while the other suites raise it to z=0.912, so the same gripper
    height reads 0.205 in one suite and 0.994 in another; a world-frame answer would
    encode which suite a frame came from.
  * train_expert_only is False EVERYWHERE, including the vanilla baselines. It
    defaults to True and freezes the VLM -- the first attempt had every auxiliary
    stuck at CE 11.0, which is ln(49280), exactly chance. Unfreezing only the
    auxiliary runs would confound "the auxiliary helped" with "the VLM was
    trainable".
  * LAP is the one setting with knowledge insulation on (flow-matching gradient cut
    off from the VLM). Its higher action loss, 0.0465, is expected and it is not
    strictly comparable to the others.
  * Action loss barely separates the settings (0.0248 vs 0.0249-0.0297) and the
    two vanilla seeds differ by 0.008 on their own. That is reconstruction on
    TRAINED embodiments, a different question from the transfer this experiment
    asks about. The answer is in your evaluation, not here.
