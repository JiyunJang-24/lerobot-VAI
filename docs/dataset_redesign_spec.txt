================================================================================
eef_pairs — WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE CURRENT DATASET, AND WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD
every claim below is a measurement from this repo, with the number that produced it
written 2026-09-04
================================================================================

The synthetic data does its intended job well: 56 embodiments posed identically lets
a model learn Cartesian motion that transfers to unseen morphologies (0.67 cm on
held-out embodiments against a 4.66 cm baseline). The problems below are all about
what happens when that knowledge has to leave the synthetic domain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 1 — ONLY 4 FIXED CAMERA POSES.  This is the dominant failure.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED
  Where the gripper lands in the image, per view, over the whole 144-pose subset:

      view 0   mean pixel (108,  64)    std (12.5, 36.0)
      view 1   mean pixel ( 50, 127)    std (14.8, 24.3)
      view 2   mean pixel ( 62, 152)    std (15.0, 18.8)
      view 3   mean pixel (245,  61)    std (22.5, 28.8)

  Four tight clusters ~200 px apart, each only 12-22 px wide horizontally. The model
  never sees the gripper anywhere else.

  Consequences, all measured:
    * the EEF-pixel head reaches 3-5 px on embodiments it has NEVER seen, but
      collapses to near chance on the policy corpus -- a fifth camera geometry
    * perturbing a synthetic frame and measuring how far its FEATURE moves, as a
      fraction of the distance a real pose change produces:
          different background ............  1%
          furniture recolour ..............  2%
          DIFFERENT ROBOT .................  4%
          shift 40 px (size unchanged) .... 31%
          zoom out to 0.75 ................ 41%
          a policy-corpus frame ........... 102%  (fully decorrelated)
      The tower treats a 40-pixel camera shift as a third of the way to an unrelated
      image while treating a different robot as 4%. The invariance that was trained
      is to embodiment and appearance; it is emphatically NOT to camera geometry.
    * this is also the best explanation for the one failure in the real-robot
      transfer: pre-training improves DIRECTION on a novel arm (0.712 -> 0.756,
      seed ranges separated) but not MAGNITUDE (2.82 -> 2.90, overlapping). How far
      something moved in pixels depends on how big the arm looks and how it is
      framed, which is exactly what a new morphology changes.

  Also: background_index is NOT independent of the camera. bg mod 4 == view exactly,
  so "12 backgrounds" is really 4 camera views x 3 backdrop colours. Backdrop
  diversity is 3.

  And the four views are not interchangeable. Over 600 pose pairs on a fixed robot,
  the SAME 3-D displacement produces pixel displacements whose magnitude varies by a
  median factor of 1.6 across views, individual cases up to 7x -- a 0.20 m motion
  moves the gripper 6 px in view 0 and 43 px in view 3. With a world-frame label the
  model must identify the view before it can use the image, and four is small enough
  to memorise.

WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD -- three options, and the choice depends on the LABEL FRAME
  Within a pair the camera must ALWAYS be identical; otherwise camera motion and
  robot motion are mixed and the label is not recoverable. Between samples:

  A. Match the deployment camera exactly (RoboCasa robot0_agentview_right, which is
     base-mounted). One camera everywhere. Best when the target setup is known and
     singular, which here it is: it removes the domain gap instead of asking the
     model to bridge it. Note today's 4 arbitrary views match nothing, which is the
     worst of both worlds.
  B. Vary the camera continuously AND express the delta in the CAMERA frame. A new
     camera is then just a new frame and nothing is memorised per view. Because the
     target camera is base-mounted, a camera-frame label is also close to the
     base-frame action the policy needs. Best when the data must serve setups not yet
     chosen.
  C. Vary the camera but keep world-frame labels -- do NOT do this. It is the current
     design with more views and it makes the task ambiguous.

  Either way, decouple background from camera, vary distance/FOV so apparent SIZE
  varies (apparent size is what magnitude transfer fails on), and
  Record the camera extrinsics and intrinsics as columns. The current export has
  none, which is why the gripper pixel could not be recomputed for the policy corpus
  and had to be re-exported separately.

  This one change is worth more than more embodiments or more backgrounds -- those
  axes are already flat at 1-4%.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 2 — POSES COVER THE WORKSPACE INSTEAD OF FORMING TRAJECTORY SEGMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED
  144 poses, pairwise distance p10 0.078 m / median 0.181 / p90 0.342.
  Restricting to pairs within 0.15 m still gives a MEAN ROTATION OF 48 degrees.
  A real trajectory over the matching horizon (25 frames, 0.09 m) rotates ~6.5.

  Fitting language thresholds to each corpus independently:
      translation  synthetic 0.028 / 0.073 / 0.110 m   real 0.026 / 0.072 / 0.107 m
                   -> agree to 7%.  "move forward moderately" means the same thing.
      rotation     synthetic 9.7 / 29.2 / 63.8 deg     real 1.0 / 3.9 / 8.8 deg
                   -> 7x apart. "roll left moderately" means 29 deg or 3.9 deg
                      depending on which corpus you are in.

  So translation transfers by construction and rotation cannot.

WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD
  What the original brief actually described: sample a reachable s_t, then sample a
  LOCAL delta_eef with the translation AND rotation magnitudes drawn to match real
  trajectory segments, compute s_t+h, render both. Pairs, not a pose grid.
  Target roughly |d| ~ 0.10 m with rotation ~5-10 deg, and include a tail of larger
  motions rather than centring on them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 3 — ROTATION IS BELOW THE VISUAL RESOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED
  Frames are 180x320. The gripper spans roughly 20 px.
  A 5-degree rotation moves the gripper's extremities by 0.87 px.
  SigLIP's patch is 16 px at 512x512, i.e. 10 px in the original frame.
  So a realistic rotation changes the image by about one ELEVENTH of one patch.

  It shows up exactly as predicted. Relative error (prediction error / actual motion):
      synthetic, ~60 deg rotations ....  24%   learned
      real,      ~6.5 deg rotations ...  95%   not learned, in 6 of 6 runs, and worse
                                               than predicting no rotation at all
  And half the real rotation is not even signal: over 25 frames the gripper travels
  12.3 degrees of path but ends 6.5 degrees from where it started (ratio 0.53).
  Translation's equivalent ratio is 0.88.

WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD
  Any one of: higher resolution (512x512 native rather than upsampled 180x320);
  a wrist camera, where the gripper fills the frame; or a longer horizon so rotation
  accumulates past the noise. Without one of these, rotation supervision is wasted
  compute no matter how the data is sampled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 4 — THE GRIPPER IS BINARY, AND ENCODED AS A SUBSET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED / OBSERVED
  Gripper state is not a column. It is which of `_closed` / `_open` a frame lives in,
  so it has exactly 2 values and a pair can only express "opened", "closed" or "no
  change". Real grippers are continuous, and the policy corpus stores them as such.
  It also forces the loader to pair ACROSS subsets to get any gripper supervision at
  all, which is easy to get wrong: pairing across `_furniture` too repaints the
  kitchen mid-motion.

WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD
  A continuous `gripper` column in observation.state, sampled over its range, in ONE
  subset. Keep `_furniture` as a separate axis if it is useful, but it should be a
  COLUMN as well, not a directory -- the same is true of the colour variant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 5 — THE SCENE IS EMPTY; THE POLICY CORPUS IS A CLUTTERED KITCHEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS MEASURED
  A policy-corpus frame sits 102% of the way from a synthetic frame to a completely
  unrelated image -- i.e. fully decorrelated. Whatever the tower encodes about
  synthetic renders carries nothing to real frames.
  The renders are a large centred robot on a checkerboard floor; the policy frames
  are a kitchen with a sink, objects, occlusion, and the arm often half out of frame.

WHAT TO RENDER INSTEAD
  Render the arms INTO scenes resembling the target domain -- the RoboCasa kitchens
  the policy data uses -- with objects on the counter and the arm sometimes partly
  out of frame. This is a bigger change than the others and is the natural companion
  to priority 1: geometry and scene content are the two axes on which the synthetic
  and real domains actually differ.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRIORITY 6 — embodiment_index HAS NO NAME MAPPING (cheap to fix, blocks analysis)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `observation.embodiment_index` is an integer with no key anywhere in the export --
  not in meta/, not in the dataset card. So held-out splits are random and
  "unseen ARM" cannot be separated from "unseen GRIPPER".
  The experiment brief asked for exactly those three categories, and they could only
  be built on visual_robust_new_barx_ur5e, which does name its six embodiments.
  Ship a meta/embodiments.json mapping index -> {arm, gripper}. This costs nothing
  and immediately sharpens every held-out result in the project.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS ALREADY RIGHT — KEEP IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * every embodiment posed at IDENTICAL EEF states. This is what makes embodiment
    identity carry zero information about the motion label, so the shortcut is
    impossible by construction rather than by sampling luck. It is the single best
    property of this dataset -- do not lose it when moving to sampled pairs. Sample
    the pose pairs ONCE and render all embodiments at them.
  * the colour variants. Verified consistent across all four subsets, and they are
    genuine nuisance-appearance augmentation on the robot itself.
  * eef_pixel and eef_in_frame. Keep them, and add the camera parameters that would
    let them be recomputed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF ONLY ONE THING CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Randomise the camera pose (priority 1). Every measurement points at it, it is the
  cheapest of the structural fixes, and the axes people would intuitively add first
  -- more embodiments, more backgrounds -- are already measured flat at 1-4%.
