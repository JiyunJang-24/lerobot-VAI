================================================================================
EXPERIMENT 1 -- SELECTIVE VISUAL ALIGNMENT: RESULTS
selfws_v2/kitchen, 24/8 scene split, arm-level embodiment holdout
Run 2026-09-13. Plan in outputs/EXP1_PLAN.txt
================================================================================

VERDICT: GO -- but only for D and E. Alignment ALONE (B, C) fails condition 2.

  The three go/no-go conditions were: retrieval improves, EEF survives, scene
  and object survive. Global contrastive alignment passes the first and FAILS
  the second -- it makes metric EEF information WORSE than the untrained
  backbone. That is the failure mode the research note anticipated, and it
  showed up exactly where it was predicted to.

  Adding the EEF preservation term fixes it. Adding the scene anchor on top
  recovers scene fidelity at some cost to pose discrimination.

================================================================================
2. THE THREE CONDITIONS, ONE AT A TIME
================================================================================

2.1 DOES CROSS-EMBODIMENT RETRIEVAL IMPROVE?   YES, for every trained method.

  The best-powered held-out-embodiment cell is seen_scene/unseen_emb (119
  states, 714 queries, s.e. ~0.041):

      A 0.550  ->  B 0.685  C 0.727  D 0.730  E 0.776

  E beats the frozen backbone by 5.5 standard errors. Selective (C) beats
  global (B) by 1.0 s.e. -- the right sign, not yet decisive.

  On the headline unseen x unseen cell everything improves too (0.663 -> 0.75
  to 0.84) but there are only 41 states, s.e. ~0.062, so the ordering AMONG
  the trained methods there is noise. C's 0.841 and D's 0.752 are 1.4 s.e.
  apart. Do not read that cell as "C is best".

2.2 IS EEF STATE PRESERVED?   ONLY WITH THE EXPLICIT TERM. This is the result.

      frozen           24.06 cm   100.4 deg
      B global         27.35 cm   118.2 deg     <-- WORSE than doing nothing
      C selective      24.25 cm    82.6 deg     <-- a wash on translation
      D +EEF           13.85 cm    20.6 deg     <-- 1.7x / 4.9x better
      E +EEF+anchor    19.69 cm    28.6 deg

  Global contrastive alignment buys its retrieval by discarding metric pose.
  It can tell WHICH of six states it is looking at (0.797 R@1) while having
  LESS linearly decodable information about where the gripper actually is than
  an untrained SigLIP. Retrieval and state preservation are genuinely
  different objectives and one does not imply the other.

  Masking the robot region (C) recovers rotation (100 -> 83 deg) but not
  translation. Only the explicit head does both.

  CAVEAT, stated plainly: D and E are the only methods that received EEF
  supervision, so this comparison is not information-matched. What it
  establishes is that the term is NECESSARY, not that those methods are
  cleverer. B and C had every opportunity to keep the information and did not.

2.3 IS SCENE / OBJECT INFORMATION DESTROYED?   No, but B and C do dent it.

  8-way scene probe (held-out kitchens, ~30 items/class, well conditioned):
      A 0.986   B 0.946   C 0.946   D 0.986   E 0.986
  24-way scene probe (~10 items/class, thin but directionally clear):
      A 0.838   B 0.635   C 0.622   D 0.784   E 0.770

  So alignment alone costs about 0.21 of 24-way scene accuracy and the
  EEF/anchor terms recover most of it. Nothing collapses.

  Object state, measured on LIBERO because selfws has no object label that is
  not 1:1 with the scene: every trained method is ABOVE the frozen backbone
  (+0.634 -> +0.67 to +0.82 median R^2). Object information is not merely
  preserved, it improves. This is a cross-domain probe -- the encoders were
  trained on RoboCasa kitchens -- so all methods pay the same domain cost and
  only the comparison between them is meaningful.


================================================================================
3. TWO THINGS WORTH UNDERSTANDING
================================================================================

3.1 THE FROZEN BASELINE RETRIEVES AT 0.663 WITH NEGATIVE POSE DISCRIMINATION

  -0.031 means same-state-different-robot pairs are, on average, LESS similar
  than different-state-same-scene pairs. Yet it retrieves at 4x chance. Both
  are true because retrieval takes an argmax, not a mean: the frozen tower's
  embodiment probe is 0.689, so inside a scene its nearest neighbours are the
  other renders of the SAME ARM FAMILY -- three of the six candidates are Jaco
  -- and among those the same state happens to be closest. It is matching
  appearance within arm family, not pose.

  Every trained method flips pose discrimination positive (+0.15 to +0.51).
  That flip, not the retrieval number, is the evidence that the mechanism
  changed.

3.2 WHAT E'S ANCHOR ACTUALLY TRADES

  E has the best held-out-embodiment retrieval (0.776), full scene fidelity
  (0.986), and the LOWEST pose discrimination of the trained methods (+0.145)
  together with the HIGHEST embodiment probe (0.662, against C's 0.378). The
  anchor holds the encoder near the frozen teacher outside the robot mask, and
  the frozen teacher carries embodiment identity -- so E keeps more of what it
  was told not to move. Whether that is a feature or a leak depends on what the
  policy needs; it is not free.


================================================================================
4. WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
================================================================================
  * ONE SEED. No method-to-method difference below ~0.05 R@1 should be trusted.
  * 41 states in the headline unseen x unseen cell. The ranking among trained
    methods there is not resolvable; only the gap to frozen is.
  * The held-out arms are Jaco and VX300SMobile. VX300SMobile reaches only 20-34%
    of states, so the held-out groups are drawn from a more compact workspace
    (20.2 cm median separation against 31.1 cm). Matched as far as the data
    allows, not perfectly.
  * Nothing here says a policy can USE this. That is the next experiment, and
    the prior evidence is not encouraging: a frozen contrastive tower matched a
    stock one exactly in the dual-tower DP runs (0.0388 vs 0.0387).
  * The counterfactual is exact for the EEF (cross-embodiment std 1.6 mm) and
    approximate for objects, which diverge after contact because each arm's
    contact dynamics differ.


================================================================================
5. THREE MEASUREMENT ARTIFACTS THAT WERE FIXED, AND WHY THEY MATTER
================================================================================
  Each of these produced a plausible table that was wrong. Recording them
  because the same traps apply to any follow-up.

  (1) UNEQUAL GALLERIES. 24 train scenes against 8 held-out, 20 train
      embodiments against 10, so held-out galleries were a third the size and
      held-out groups scored HIGHER: frozen 0.733 on unseen/unseen against
      0.618 on seen/seen. Fixed by equalising states and embodiments per group.

  (2) UNEQUAL STATE SPACING. Held-out-embodiment groups can only use states
      VX300SMobile reaches, which cluster: the frozen baseline's misses landed
      4.1 cm away there against 20 cm in the seen groups. It was answering an
      easier question. Fixed with a 5 cm minimum EEF separation within a scene.

  (3) THE SCENE SHORTCUT. Across scenes, recognising the kitchen narrows the
      gallery to the two states that kitchen contributed, and the rest is a coin
      flip -- which is how the frozen tower scored 0.830 with NEGATIVE pose
      discrimination. Retrieval is now within-scene, where every candidate
      shares the background and the shortcut pays nothing.

  Also: the LIBERO object probe first returned R^2 = -2853. That was 768
  features regressed on 28 rows with near-zero-variance target dimensions
  exploding the denominator, not a finding. Now 240 rows per shard, dead
  dimensions dropped, ridge alpha chosen on held-out training rows, median over
  dimensions instead of mean.

  policy integration, two cheap things worth doing first:

     instead of Jaco + VX300SMobile). The current headline cell is too small
     to separate C, D and E, and the held-out arms are the two least typical.

  2. POINT tools/analyze_jaco_representation.py AT THE D CHECKPOINT. It already
     measures exactly this property on REAL policy data with a scene control,
     and it takes minutes. If D's advantage survives there, the policy
     experiment is worth its cost; if it does not, the 102% cross-domain gap is
     still the binding constraint and no amount of alignment on synthetic
     renders will fix it.

7.1 POSE DISCRIMINATION AND RETRIEVAL COME APART, AND THE SPLIT IS INFORMATIVE

  C has the HIGHEST pose discrimination of anything measured (+0.893) and nearly
  the WORST retrieval of the trained methods (0.487). D has slightly lower
  discrimination (+0.827) and the best retrieval by a wide margin (0.838).

  The two metrics ask different questions. Pose discrimination is a MEAN over
  pairs: does same-state sit above same-scene-different-state on average? C
  answers yes emphatically. Retrieval is an ARGMAX: among 240 candidates, is the
  exact matching state the single closest? For that, similarity has to be ordered
  finely enough to separate nearby poses -- and C's linear EEF probe was 24.2 cm,
  no better than an untrained backbone. It knows this frame is a different pose
  from that one without knowing which pose either is, so nearby states blur and
  the argmax lands on the wrong one.

  This is the SAME finding as section 2.2, showing up independently on real images
  through a different metric. The EEF preservation term is what converts alignment
  into usable retrieval.

  The old eef_pairs tower fails the same way and worse: +0.485 discrimination,
  0.375 retrieval, and its top-1 miss is 27.4 cm off. It separates poses without
  being able to find them.

7.2 D BEATS EVERYTHING PREVIOUSLY BUILT, ON REAL FRAMES

  Against the stock tower: 4.6x the pose discrimination (+0.827 vs +0.179) AND
  better retrieval (0.838 vs 0.625). Against the old eef_pairs tower: 1.7x the
  discrimination and 2.2x the retrieval.

  The old tower could not beat stock on retrieval at all -- 0.375 against 0.625 --
  because stock wins that by scene matching (its same-scene-different-state score
  is +0.369, so more than half of its apparent similarity is the kitchen). D wins
  on both while taking almost nothing from the background (+0.045).

  Being Jaco costs D 0.065 of similarity, against 0.172 for B and 0.131 for C.
  The method with the state term also generalises best to the unfamiliar arm.

7.3 WHAT CHANGED VERSUS THE OLD TOWER

  Same objective family, same backbone, same batch shape. Two differences:
    * the training corpus has a SCENE AXIS (32 distinct kitchens against a flat
      coloured checkerboard floor), and objects in the frame
    * an explicit EEF preservation term

  Section 2.2 shows the second one is doing most of the work on state metrics, and
  7.1 shows it is what makes retrieval work. The first is what makes any of it
  transfer to a real kitchen at all.

  Same 24/8 kitchen split, but Panda and IIWA held out instead of Jaco and
  VX300SMobile. This is the harsher test of the headline claim, because Panda and
  IIWA are TYPICAL arms -- the frozen backbone already handles them well
  (36.7 deg rotation error against 100.4 deg on split 1), so there is much less
  room to improve and the EEF comparison is far less forgiving.

  THE HEADLINE FINDING REPLICATES, and more sharply:
      * B is WORSE than the frozen backbone on BOTH EEF axes again -- 28.8 cm vs
        23.1, and 72.4 deg vs 36.7. Alignment alone degrading state information is
        not a split-1 accident.
      * C is worse than frozen on rotation too here (61.5 vs 36.7). On split 1 it
        looked like masking recovered rotation; against an easier frozen baseline
        it does not. Masking alone is not sufficient.
      * D is the only method better than frozen on BOTH axes in BOTH splits.
      * The 24-way scene probe repeats its pattern exactly: B dents it most
        (0.910 -> 0.795), C partly (0.846), D and E exceed the frozen backbone

  Retrieval saturates on this split (all trained methods 0.94-0.97 on
  unseen x unseen) because the held-out arms are ordinary. Read the EEF and scene
  columns here, not the retrieval ones.

  NOTE ON CROSS-SPLIT READING: group difficulty depends on WHICH arms land in
  which group, so numbers are not comparable between split 1 and split 2 -- on
  split 2 the "seen embodiment" groups contain Jaco and VX300SMobile, which is why
  the frozen baseline scores 0.456 there and 0.799 on the held-out side. Only
  method-to-method comparisons WITHIN a split mean anything.

9.1 WHAT SURVIVES
  Retrieval is stable to within 0.04, confirming the earlier warning not to trust
  differences below ~0.05. Pose discrimination is stable to within 0.02 -- C
  +0.510/+0.509, D +0.328/+0.319, E +0.145/+0.124 -- so the ordering there is
  real. D's rotation error is 20.6 and 20.8 deg, essentially identical.

9.2 WHAT DOES NOT, AND THE CLAIM IT WEAKENS
  C's ROTATION ERROR SWINGS 82.6 -> 48.0 deg BETWEEN SEEDS. That 35 deg spread is
  larger than the entire C-to-D gap measured on seed 0 alone. The direction holds
  in both seeds (D beats C: 20.6 < 82.6, and 20.8 < 48.0), but "C is at 83 deg"
  was over-precise and should be read as "C is somewhere between 50 and 85 deg,
  and unstable".

  Means over the two seeds:
      A  24.06 cm / 100.4 deg     C  22.54 cm / 65.3 deg
      D  15.08 cm /  20.7 deg     E  18.31 cm / 27.9 deg
  So C barely improves translation over doing nothing (22.5 vs 24.1) and D is
  1.6x better on translation and 4.9x on rotation.

  B WAS RUN AT ONE SEED ONLY on this split. Given C's variance, "B at 118 deg is
  worse than frozen at 100 deg" cannot rest on that alone. It rests instead on the
  SECOND SPLIT, where B is worse than frozen on both axes by a wide margin
  (28.80 vs 23.09 cm, 72.4 vs 36.7 deg). Two independent splits, same direction,
  both axes. That is the evidence for the headline claim; the single-seed split-1
  number is corroboration, not proof.

9.3 THE REVISED SUMMARY
  Retrieval improves for EVERY alignment method and does NOT distinguish them:
  across seeds, C 0.741, D 0.731, E 0.757 on the held-out-arm cell, all within
  noise of each other and all far above frozen's 0.550.

  What distinguishes the methods is state and scene preservation, and there only
  the explicit term matters. If the goal were retrieval alone, plain global
  contrastive alignment would be enough. It is not enough for anything that needs
  to know where the gripper is.
