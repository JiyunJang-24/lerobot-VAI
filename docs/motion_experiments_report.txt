THE HYPOTHESIS UNDER TEST
-------------------------
If a model learns to read the SAME Cartesian EEF motion across many synthetically
rendered robot morphologies, that visual-motion knowledge may transfer to held-out
embodiments and carry task knowledge to robots with no demonstrations.

Split into three questions that can fail independently:

  Q1  can visual motion knowledge generalise to unseen robot morphologies?
  Q2  does increasing synthetic embodiment diversity improve that generalisation?
  Q3  does that knowledge improve task transfer to robots without demonstrations?

SUMMARY OF FINDINGS
-------------------
  Q1  YES.  0.67 cm translation error on 14 embodiments never trained on, against a
            4.66 cm predict-the-mean baseline. Direction cosine 0.980.
  Q2  YES, and it is morphology diversity rather than data volume. Held-out error
            falls 4.3x from 2 to 42 embodiments and has not flattened. Holding the
            total sample count FIXED reproduces the curve exactly.
  Q3  CANNOT BE ANSWERED with the current evaluation. Not a negative result -- the
            measurement has no resolution. Details in section 5.

1. DATASETS
================================================================================

1.1 SYNTHETIC MOTION DATA -- /dataset/jiyun/dataset_git/eef_pairs/
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Four subsets, used together:
    56combo_48_bg12_closed
    56combo_48_bg12_open
    56combo_48_bg12_closed_furniture
    56combo_48_bg12_open_furniture

Structure: 56 embodiments x 48 canonical EEF poses x 12 backgrounds x 4 camera
views. One rendered frame per row, 312,176 rows total. Each episode_index IS one
pose -- verified, the state std within an episode is 3e-5.

observation.state is [9]:  xyz(0:3) + quaternion_xyzw(3:7) + eef_pixel(7:9)
  (state[7:9] was verified identical to the observation.eef_pixel column, max
   difference exactly 0.0)

WHY NO NEW RENDERING WAS NEEDED
All 56 embodiments are posed at the SAME 48 canonical EEF states. So a motion
defined as "pose i -> pose j" is literally the same motion on every robot. The
brief asked for a shared motion DISTRIBUTION across embodiments; this is stronger.
One pool of pose pairs is drawn once and reused for every embodiment, so embodiment
identity carries exactly zero information about the label. That makes the
identity-as-shortcut failure impossible by construction rather than by sampling
luck, and assert_no_motion_shortcut() checks it at every startup.

MOTION SCALE
The 48 poses are 0.009 - 0.307 m apart pairwise. Pairs are filtered to <= 0.15 m
("local motions"), leaving 1262 pairs with mean displacement 0.096 m.

1.2 REAL TASK DATA -- dataset_git/barx_panda_ur5e_iiwa/
--------------------------------------------------------
Three trees, one task (PnPSinkToCounter), three embodiments:
    IIWAOmron   500,288 frames / 1000 episodes
    PandaOmron  446,542 frames /  900 episodes
    UR5eOmron   498,810 frames / 1000 episodes

Camera used: observation.images.robot0_agentview_right, 180x320, base-mounted.
observation.state is [16]: base pose(0:7), eef xyz(7:10), eef quat_wxyz(10:14),
gripper(14:16). The layout is not documented anywhere; it was recovered from
variance structure and is recorded in CLAUDE.md section 1.

1.3 THE TWO CONVENTION TRAPS
-----------------------------
(a) QUATERNION ORDER IS OPPOSITE BETWEEN THE TWO CORPORA.
    eef_pairs is xyzw. The barx trees are wxyz (verified: the first component sits
    near 1.0 for a near-identity rotation, and the norm is exactly 1).
    motion_data.py is xyzw throughout and is the only place the conversion happens.

(b) THE 12-DIM ACTION IS NOT A METRIC DISPLACEMENT.
    action[12] = [base(3), torso, mode, dpos(3), drot(3), gripper]
    a[0:4] are identically zero and a[4] is -1 (mode) in these trees, so only 7 of
    12 dimensions carry signal. dpos/drot are NORMALISED OSC controller commands.

    Regressing the achieved base-frame EEF delta on the commanded dpos:
        IIWA   R2 = 0.47   ~0.007 m per unit
        Panda  R2 = 0.31   ~0.006 m per unit
        UR5e   R2 = 0.71   ~0.010 m per unit
    A different near-diagonal matrix per robot and less than half the variance
    explained. Using the action as the cross-embodiment motion target would bake
    per-robot controller gains into the label -- which is exactly the embodiment
    shortcut the hypothesis is trying to avoid.

    EVERY MOTION TARGET IN THESE EXPERIMENTS IS THE MEASURED STATE DELTA INSTEAD,
    expressed in the robot BASE frame. The camera is base-mounted, so image and
    label stay in a fixed relationship.

1.4 REQUIRED SANITY TEST (runs at every startup)
-------------------------------------------------
verify_reconstruction() asserts that applying the computed delta to s_t reproduces
s_t+h:
    position:  p_i + d_pos  ->  p_j        max error  0.00e+00 m
    rotation:  q_rel (x) q_i ->  q_j       max error  7.07e-08   (sign-invariant,
                                           because q and -q are one rotation)


================================================================================
2. MODEL AND TRAINING
================================================================================

Deliberately minimal -- the question is the data, not the architecture.

    image_t   --\
                  SigLIP tower (shared weights) -> attention pool -\
    image_t+h --/                                                   concat -> MLP
                                                                     |
                                    delta_position (3) + delta_quaternion (4)
                                    + gripper change (3-class)

Backbone: the SmolVLM SigLIP vision tower, the same one the policies use.
Pooling:  attention pooling, not mean. A mean over 1024 position-tagged patches is
          close to location-blind (measured, CLAUDE.md section 3), and a
          displacement is nothing but a change of location.
Losses:   position MSE on normalised delta; rotation 1 - |<q_pred, q_target>|
          (never MSE -- that punishes a correct prediction carrying the other
          sign); gripper cross-entropy weighted 0.1.
Training: 6000 steps, batch 24 pairs (48 images), bf16 autocast, tower lr 1e-5,
          head lr 1e-4.

LEAKAGE GUARD: assert_no_leakage() rejects any overlap between train and held-out
embodiments, and the sampled training pool is re-checked for held-out ids before
the first step.

3. EXPERIMENT 1 -- DOES MOTION UNDERSTANDING GENERALISE ACROSS MORPHOLOGY?
================================================================================

SETUP    42 training embodiments, 14 held out at random.
         Held out: 0, 1, 3, 8, 12, 14, 23, 27, 28, 33, 34, 36, 42, 49

HOW IT WAS EVALUATED
  96 freshly sampled pairs per embodiment, scored per embodiment and then averaged
  within each split, so a single odd robot cannot dominate. Reported metrics:
    translation MAE          metres, the direct error
    translation direction    cosine between predicted and true displacement --
                             separates "right direction, wrong magnitude" from
                             "wrong entirely"
    rotation error           geodesic degrees, sign-invariant
    gripper accuracy         3-class: opened / unchanged / closed

BASELINES (the numbers below are meaningless without these)
    predict the mean displacement every time    4.66 cm
    predict no rotation at all                  60.2 deg

RESULTS
    split       trans MAE    dir cos    rot err    grip acc
    seen          0.41 cm      0.993      8.2 deg     0.992
    HELD-OUT      0.67 cm      0.980     10.8 deg     0.970

    per held-out embodiment (translation MAE):
      emb  0  0.56 cm     emb 27  0.67 cm
      emb  1  0.49 cm     emb 28  0.83 cm
      emb  3  0.55 cm     emb 33  0.52 cm
      emb  8  0.41 cm     emb 34  0.52 cm
      emb 12  1.00 cm     emb 36  0.66 cm
      emb 14  0.92 cm     emb 42  0.84 cm
      emb 23  0.74 cm     emb 49  0.70 cm

READING
  7x better than predict-the-mean on robots never seen, with the direction
  essentially correct. The seen -> held-out gap is small (0.41 -> 0.67 cm), so this
  is generalisation and not recall. No held-out embodiment fails; the worst is
  1.00 cm.
  => Q1 IS YES.

4. EXPERIMENT 2 -- DOES EMBODIMENT DIVERSITY HELP, OR IS IT JUST MORE DATA?
================================================================================

The same 14 embodiments are held out at every rung, so the test set never moves.
Two sampling protocols, because one alone cannot separate the two explanations:

  PROTOCOL A   4000 pairs per embodiment -> total data GROWS with embodiment count
  PROTOCOL B   60,000 pairs total regardless -> total data FIXED

RESULTS (held-out embodiments)

  n_emb |  A samples  A trans MAE  A rot  |  B samples  B trans MAE  B rot
  ------+--------------------------------+--------------------------------
      2 |     8,000      3.61 cm  52.9deg |    60,000      3.56 cm  55.1deg
      4 |    16,000      2.54 cm  32.2deg |    60,000      2.34 cm  33.0deg
      8 |    32,000      1.73 cm  27.2deg |    60,000      1.79 cm  27.9deg
     16 |    64,000      1.20 cm  17.7deg |    60,000      1.16 cm  16.5deg
     42 |   168,000      0.74 cm  11.1deg |    60,000      0.84 cm  12.0deg

  direction cosine, held out:   n=2 0.52/0.57   n=4 0.77/0.80   n=8 0.89/0.89
                                n=16 0.93/0.93  n=42 0.98/0.97   (A/B)

READING
  Protocol A ends with 2.8x the data of protocol B and the two curves are
  indistinguishable at every rung. The gain therefore comes from MORPHOLOGY
  DIVERSITY, not from having more images. Error falls 4.3x from 2 to 42
  embodiments and has not flattened, so more embodiments is still the right lever.

  THE CONTROL THAT MAKES THIS READABLE: seen-split error moves the OTHER WAY,
  0.27 cm at n=2 rising to 0.46 cm at n=42. Few embodiments is not an easier
  problem -- it is memorisation. The model fits its own two robots better and
  transfers worse.
  => Q2 IS YES, AND IT IS DIVERSITY.

5. EXPERIMENT 3 -- DOES IT HELP TASK TRANSFER? (AND WHY WE CANNOT TELL)
================================================================================

POLICY   the language-conditioned Diffusion Policy already in the repo, chosen over
         a VLA on purpose: a VLM failing to interpret a separately learned
         representation would be an extra confound.

THREE ARMS, identical stock tower / batch 32 / 15,000 steps, differing ONLY in the
auxiliary:
    A  vanilla              (image_t, task) -> action
    B  real motion aux      + (image_t, image_t+25) -> delta_EEF on real trajectories
    C  synthetic + real     + the same objective on 42 synthetic embodiments

UR5eOmron was excluded from task training ENTIRELY, then scored -- so "a robot with
no task demonstrations" is a real condition and not a label.

HORIZON MATCHING: real motion over 25 steps at 20 fps is 0.09 m, matching the
synthetic 0.094 m. This was measured, not guessed.

RESULTS (action loss)
    arm                  IIWA(seen)  Panda(seen)  UR5e(HELD OUT)  final motion loss
    A vanilla               0.0316       0.0368        0.0355       --
    B real motion aux       0.0332       0.0379        0.0376       1.61 (from 7.45)
    C synthetic + real      0.0331       0.0377        0.0373       1.79 (from 4.50)

The auxiliary was genuinely training -- its loss fell 4.6x and 2.5x. It is not a
dead term. And it bought nothing; A is marginally best everywhere.

BUT THE CONTROLS SHOW THE MEASUREMENT CANNOT ANSWER THE QUESTION
    UR5e action loss WITH its own demonstrations (D_headroom)   0.0348
    UR5e action loss WITHOUT any                (A_vanilla)     0.0355
    ------------------------------------------------------------------
    TOTAL HEADROOM -- what a UR5e demonstration is worth         0.0007

    seed-to-seed spread, same arm (C vs C seed 1)                0.0021
    the A-vs-C difference the null was about                     0.0018

  The noise is 3x the entire headroom, and larger than the effect being claimed.
  Even a perfect method could only have moved 0.0007, which is a third of the
  run-to-run variation. Second seeds also FLIP the seen-robot ordering (A_vanilla
  IIWA 0.0316 -> 0.0361, Panda 0.0368 -> 0.0337), which is the same warning again.

  => EXPERIMENT 3 IS NOT EVIDENCE AGAINST THE HYPOTHESIS. It is a measurement with
     no resolution. Reporting it as "synthetic motion supervision does not help"
     would be wrong.

WHY THE HEADROOM IS SO SMALL (structural, not a bug)
  The action space is a normalised delta convention shared by all three robots and
  the task motion is nearly identical for each, so predicting UR5e actions barely
  requires having seen UR5e. A metric a held-out robot already scores well on
  cannot show that transfer improved.

CORROBORATION: the four earlier DP integration runs say the same thing. Step-matched,
frozen / finetune / scratch / online all sit within 0.0005 of each other
(0.0347-0.0352 at 10k steps). One 'online' log line read 0.0247 and looked like a
real gain until it was step-matched -- it was a single noisy batch. Single log lines
are not results.

  Figure: outputs/exp3_headroom.png

6. WHAT IS NOT DONE, AND WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN NEXT
================================================================================

6.1 NO TASK-SUCCESS EVALUATION EXISTS ANYWHERE IN THIS REPO
    robosuite / robocasa / mujoco are not installed; cfg.env has never been set in
    any run here, so pc_success has never been computed. Every number in this
    document is action loss or a feature statistic.
    THIS IS NOW THE HIGHEST-VALUE MISSING PIECE. Q1 and Q2 got clean answers
    precisely because motion prediction has a real metric. Q3 does not, and no
    amount of further training will change that.

6.2 THE ROTATION DISTRIBUTIONS DO NOT MATCH
    real trajectory over 25 steps   ~9 degrees
    synthetic pose pairs            ~60 degrees
    The 48 poses were sampled to COVER the workspace, not to resemble trajectory
    segments. Capping at 30 degrees narrows it to 21 but costs vocabulary (1262
    pose pairs down to 270).
    PROPER FIX, at render time: sample s_t, then a LOCAL delta_eef, then render --
    which is what the original brief described. The current data approximates it.

6.3 THE THREE HELD-OUT CATEGORIES COULD NOT BE BUILT
    The brief asked for novel-arm/seen-gripper, seen-arm/novel-gripper and
    novel-both. eef_pairs' observation.embodiment_index is an integer with no name
    mapping anywhere in the export, so the split is random and those categories
    cannot be separated.
    NOTE: the visual_robust_new_barx_ur5e tree DOES name its 6 embodiments
    (UR5eOmron, PandaOmron, IIWAOmron, JacoOmron, PandaOmronPandaGripper,
    JacoOmronPandaGripper) and therefore does support the three categories --
    JacoOmronPandaGripper is precisely novel arm + seen gripper. That is the route
    to closing this gap.

6.4 EXPERIMENT 3'S HELD-OUT ROBOT IS ONE OF THREE
    Holding one of IIWA/Panda/UR5e out leaves two to train on. A corpus with more
    embodiments per task is needed for this to be a strong test.

8. THE JACO EXPERIMENT -- A NOVEL ARM, WITH REAL GROUND TRUTH
================================================================================
(added after the re-export landed; sections 1-7 predate it)

WHAT THE RE-EXPORT CHANGED
  ChiefJang/barx_panda_ur5e_iiwa and ChiefJang/visual_robust_robocasa_x now carry
  eef_pixel and eef_in_frame. Two cautions applied when taking them:

  (a) the re-export is v2.1 and the local trees are already converted to v3.0, and
  (b) the re-exported info.json for the visual-robust tree declares only THREE
      image features where the local one declares all SIX -- Jaco among the missing.

  Downloading meta/ over the local trees would therefore have reverted the
  conversion AND deleted Jaco, which is the whole point of this experiment. So only
  the new parquet was pulled, joined on (episode_index, frame_index), and the two
  columns written into the local v3.0 files. All four trees joined 100% of rows:
  54,063 / 500,288 / 446,542 / 498,810.
  (tools/merge_eef_pixel_columns.py; the hub rate limit is 1000 API requests per
  5 minutes and a 1000-file tree exceeds it, so it backs off for 60 s on a 429.)

THE DATASET
  visual_robust_new_barx_ur5e renders the SAME 108 episodes from six NAMED
  embodiments. eef_pixel is one column per FRAME, not per embodiment -- correct,
  since every render places the same EEF pose under the same camera. So the target
  is identical across the six and ONLY the appearance of the robot changes.
  80.2% of frames have the EEF in frame; the rest are excluded via eef_in_frame.

  This is what finally provides the three held-out categories the brief asked for,
  which the integer embodiment_index on eef_pairs could never support:
      PandaOmron / IIWAOmron / UR5eOmron   seen arm, own gripper
      PandaOmronPandaGripper               seen arm, seen gripper (recombined)
      JacoOmronPandaGripper                NOVEL ARM, seen gripper
      JacoOmron                            NOVEL ARM, novel gripper

EVALUATION
  641 in-frame samples from 40 episodes, every 25th frame. The last 160 are held
  out as the evaluation set for every embodiment; the two Jaco embodiments never
  appear in training at all. Metric: Euclidean pixel error against the ground-truth
  eef_pixel on a 320x180 image.

RESULT
  embodiment               category                  zero-shot   trained-on-4
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PandaOmron               seen arm, own gripper       50.7 px       11.8 px
  IIWAOmron                seen arm, own gripper       51.7 px       10.6 px
  UR5eOmron                seen arm, own gripper       55.2 px       11.7 px
  PandaOmronPandaGripper   seen arm, seen gripper      63.1 px       11.3 px
  JacoOmronPandaGripper    NOVEL ARM, seen gripper     63.5 px       15.0 px
  JacoOmron                NOVEL ARM, novel gripper    52.1 px       14.2 px
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  mean seen                                            55.2 px       11.4 px
  mean held-out Jaco                                   57.8 px       14.6 px

  "zero-shot"    = the pixel head trained on synthetic eef_pairs renders, applied
                   to these real kitchen frames with nothing adapted.
  "trained-on-4" = the same head trained on real frames of the four non-Jaco
                   embodiments only.

TWO CONCLUSIONS

  (1) IT CONFIRMS SECTION 9.5 DIRECTLY, WITH GROUND TRUTH.
      The synthetic head scores 50-63 px on real frames against 3-5 px in its own
      domain. Section 9.5 could only infer this by fitting a camera to the head's
      own predictions, because no ground-truth pixel existed on policy data. Now it
      is measured. Note that the zero-shot failure is UNIFORM across all six
      embodiments -- Jaco is not worse than Panda. The head is failing on the scene
      domain, not on morphology, which is exactly what the geometry-sensitivity
      analysis predicted.

  (2) LOCALISATION DOES TRANSFER TO A NOVEL ARM once trained in the right domain.
      11.4 px on embodiments it trained on, 14.6 px on an arm it has never seen --
      29% worse, not broken. And novel arm + novel gripper (14.2 px) is no worse
      than novel arm + seen gripper (15.0 px), so within this tree it is the ARM
      that carries the difficulty, and even that costs about 3 px.

      This is the real-data counterpart of experiments 1 and 2, which ran on
      synthetic renders: real trajectories, real cluttered kitchens, named
      morphologies.

CAVEATS
  641 samples from 40 episodes and 1500 training steps -- indicative, not
  definitive. The seen-vs-Jaco difference of ~3 px has no repeat run behind it, so
  it should not be read finer than "small". Widening to all 108 episodes and adding
  seeds is cheap and is the obvious next step.

9. TWO-IMAGE MOTION PREDICTION ON A NOVEL ARM (the Jaco motion experiment)
================================================================================

A DISTINCTION THAT IS EASY TO LOSE
  Experiments 1 and 2   two images -> the Cartesian motion between them.
                        Ran on eef_pairs only. Those embodiments are unnamed
                        integer ids and Jaco is NOT among them.
  Section 8 (pixel)     ONE image -> where is the gripper. A different task.
  This section          the missing cell: the two-image motion task, on REAL
                        trajectories, with Jaco held out.

SETUP
  Same six named embodiments, same 108 episodes. observation.state is shared by
  every render, so the motion label is IDENTICAL across all six and only the
  robot's appearance changes.
  738 pairs from 60 episodes, horizon 25 frames (1.2 s) giving 0.102 m mean
  displacement -- matched to the synthetic 0.096 m. Last 140 pairs held out as the
  evaluation set. Trained on the four non-Jaco embodiments, 2000 steps.
  The same reconstruction assertion runs here too and passes.

FRAME CAVEAT (stated, not buried)
  This tree's xyz is WORLD frame and the base is parked differently per episode, so
  one visual motion maps to different world deltas across episodes. The image does
  show the kitchen, so base orientation is inferable and the task is not ill-posed
  -- but it is harder than a base-frame target, and the absolute numbers are worse
  because of it. The confound is identical for all six embodiments, so the
  seen-vs-Jaco comparison is unaffected.

RESULTS
  embodiment               category                  trans MAE  dir cos   rotation
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PandaOmron               seen arm, own gripper       3.25 cm    0.705    9.3 deg
  IIWAOmron                seen arm, own gripper       3.22 cm    0.696    9.4 deg
  UR5eOmron                seen arm, own gripper       3.23 cm    0.715    9.3 deg
  PandaOmronPandaGripper   seen arm, seen gripper      3.48 cm    0.680    9.4 deg
  JacoOmronPandaGripper    NOVEL ARM, seen gripper     4.22 cm    0.515    9.2 deg
  JacoOmron                NOVEL ARM, novel gripper    3.57 cm    0.658    9.0 deg
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  mean seen                                            3.30 cm    0.699    9.3 deg
  mean held-out Jaco                                   3.89 cm    0.587    9.1 deg
  BASELINE: predict the training mean                  5.23 cm      --     8.8 deg
                                                                    (identity rot.)

READING -- IT WORKS, BUT WEAKLY, AND MUCH LESS WELL THAN ON SYNTHETIC DATA

  Translation is genuinely learned and does transfer: 3.30 cm on seen embodiments
  against a 5.23 cm predict-the-mean baseline, and 3.89 cm on an arm never seen.
  Direction cosine drops 0.699 -> 0.587 from seen to held out.

  But this is nowhere near experiment 1's synthetic result (0.67 cm against a
  4.66 cm baseline, direction cosine 0.980). The two are NOT comparable and the
  gap should not be read as "Jaco is hard". Three reasons, in order of likely size:
     738 training pairs here versus 168,000 there
     a world-frame target with per-episode base yaw versus a clean base-frame one
     2000 steps versus 6000

  ROTATION IS NOT LEARNED AT ALL. The model scores 9.0-9.4 deg while simply
  predicting no rotation scores 8.8 deg -- it is WORSE than the trivial baseline.
  Real trajectory rotation over 25 frames averages about 7 deg, which is a much
  smaller and subtler signal than the synthetic pose pairs' 60 deg. Any claim about
  rotation transfer would be unsupported.

  Within the held-out pair, novel arm + novel gripper (3.57 cm, cos 0.658) scores
  BETTER than novel arm + seen gripper (4.22 cm, cos 0.515). That ordering is the
  opposite of the intuitive one and rests on 140 evaluation samples with no repeat
  run, so it should be treated as noise rather than a finding.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS CONCLUSIVE
  All 108 episodes and a denser stride (738 pairs is the binding constraint), a
  base-frame target, and 2-3 seeds. All cheap. Until then this establishes that the
  two-image motion task transfers to an unseen arm in the real domain, and does not
  establish how well.
