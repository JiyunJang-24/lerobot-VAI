DEFECT REPORT — visual_robust_new_segmentation
Segmentation videos are silently truncated at generation time
Found 2026-09-16 while building a contrastive corpus from this dataset.
================================================================================

SUMMARY

  In this dataset every frame is rendered from three embodiments, and each
  (embodiment, camera) pair ships an RGB video plus a segmentation video.

  The RGB videos are always complete. The SEGMENTATION videos are frequently
  SHORTER than the episode they belong to -- about 21-23% of them. Nothing
  reports this: every worker log ends with "DONE ... queue empty, exiting", so
  the generator believes it finished cleanly.

  This is NOT a transfer problem. The SCP was verified independently (file
  counts and a per-file path/size manifest match source and destination), and
  the pattern rules it out anyway: a transfer fault would truncate RGB and
  segmentation alike, and RGB is never short.

  segmentation stops early, it does not drop frames from the middle.


THE PATTERN THAT POINTS AT THE CAUSE

  Affected episode indices cluster at 7-10 and at 66+, and work was dispatched
  in 27-episode chunks -- the log directory contains files named

      <TREE>_0_27.log   <TREE>_27_54.log   <TREE>_54_81.log   <TREE>_81_108.log

  So the truncation concentrates at the END of chunk ranges. That is consistent
  with the segmentation writer being closed/flushed before it has drained, or
  with the worker advancing to the next episode while the segmentation encoder
  still has buffered frames.

  Most likely culprits, in order:
    1. The segmentation video encoder is not flushed on close. With PyAV this is
       the classic `container.mux(stream.encode())` (flush with no argument)
       being skipped before `container.close()`; libx264 holds frames in its
       lookahead buffer and they are lost. The RGB writer is presumably flushed
       correctly, or uses a different path, which is why only segmentation is
       affected.
    2. A per-episode timeout or early `break` in the segmentation render loop
       that is not checked afterwards.
    3. Writer objects going out of scope and being garbage-collected mid-write
       at chunk boundaries.

  Hypothesis 1 explains everything observed: RGB unaffected, segmentation always
  short by a tail, worse at chunk ends, and no error reported anywhere.


WHAT TO FIX

  1. Flush the segmentation encoder before closing it, exactly as the RGB writer
     does. In PyAV:
         container.mux(stream.encode())   # <-- no argument: drains the encoder
         container.close()
  2. ASSERT AFTER WRITING. Re-open each written segmentation video and check its
     frame count equals the episode length; fail loudly if not. The absence of
     this check is why 21-23% of the corpus shipped broken with clean logs.
  3. Regenerate the segmentation videos for the affected episodes. RGB does not
     need regenerating -- it is intact.

  A useful acceptance test, which is also how this was found:

      for every (tree, embodiment, camera) and every episode:
          frames(<...>_segmentation/episode_NNNNNN.mp4) == episode_length
          frames(<...>/episode_NNNNNN.mp4)              == episode_length

  Both must hold for all 324 pairs per tree before the dataset is considered
  done. Do not rely on worker exit status; it is currently clean while the data
  is not.


NOTE ON THE OTHER DATASET

  barx3_segmentation was produced by what appears to be the same pipeline (same
  worker.sh / queue.tsv / logs_worker_gpuN.log layout). Its first 100 episodes
  per tree are verified intact -- a build over exactly that range completed with
  a hard length assertion and did not trip. Episodes beyond 100 are being
