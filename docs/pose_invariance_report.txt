================================================================================
DOES THE CONTRASTIVE BACKBONE GIVE THE SAME FEATURE FOR THE SAME EEF POSE
ON A ROBOT IT HAS NEVER SEEN?
tower: outputs/siglip_pretrain/all4_n42_all  (the one the dual-tower DP runs load)
tool:  tools/analyze_pose_invariance.py      2026-09-07
================================================================================

SHORT ANSWER
  Yes, and by a wide margin -- inside the synthetic render domain.
  Same pose, unseen robot .................... centred cosine +0.974
  Different pose, same robot ................. centred cosine -0.042
  Held-out robot -> nearest training frame ... 100% same pose, 0.0 cm error
  The stock SigLIP control scores +0.104 and 22% on the same measurements, so the
  separation is the pre-training, not something SigLIP already did.

HOW TO READ THE NUMBERS
  All cosines are CENTRED (batch mean subtracted) as well as raw. SigLIP features
  are anisotropic enough that ANY two images sit near raw 0.95, so a raw cosine of
  0.97 means nothing on its own -- look at the centred column. The stock tower makes
  this obvious: raw +0.967 for the same pose and +0.963 for a different one, a gap
  of 0.004, which centring turns into +0.116 versus -0.007.

SETUP
  56combo_48_bg12_closed, the corpus this tower was trained on. 42 embodiments were
  in training, 14 were held out entirely. 24 canonical EEF poses, 6 embodiments per
  group per pose, 288 frames. Background, camera view and colour are held FIXED in
  the headline condition so that the only thing differing between two frames is the
  pose and the robot -- otherwise a low similarity could be the backdrop.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SAME POSE, DIFFERENT ROBOT  (background / view / colour fixed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                        contrastive tower      stock (control)
                                        centred     raw        centred     raw
  same pose, train x train              +0.979    +0.982       +0.116    +0.967
  same pose, train x HELD-OUT           +0.974    +0.977       +0.104    +0.968
  same pose, HELD-OUT x HELD-OUT        +0.971    +0.975       +0.109    +0.968
  DIFFERENT pose, same embodiment       -0.042    +0.105       -0.007    +0.963

  Being held out costs 0.005 to 0.008 of cosine. Two robots the tower has NEVER
  seen, at the same pose, sit at +0.971 -- while the SAME robot at a different pose
  sits at -0.042. The representation is essentially reading pose and ignoring which
  robot it is looking at, and that behaviour does not depend on having trained on
  the robot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. RETRIEVAL: hand it a held-out robot, does it find the pose?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Each held-out frame is matched against the TRAINING frames only.

                              contrastive       stock
  same pose top-1               1.000           0.222
  same pose top-5               1.000           0.521
  median EEF error              0.0 cm          7.9 cm
  mean EEF error                0.0 cm          --

  Reported as an EEF distance as well as a hit rate, because "wrong" says nothing
  about how wrong. The contrastive tower is not approximately right; it retrieves
  the exact pose every time.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT THE INVARIANCE SURVIVES  (relaxing the nuisance axes one at a time)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  allowed to differ            train x HELD-OUT   held-out retrieval top-1
  nothing (headline)                +0.974              1.000
  background + colour               +0.969              0.993
  background + colour + camera view +0.951              0.979

  Backdrop and robot paint cost almost nothing. Camera view costs 0.023 and 2% of
  retrieval -- but note WHY it survives: all four canonical views are IN the
  training data. This is invariance across geometries it has seen, not invariance to
  geometry in general.

  THE DISTINCTION MATTERS, and it is where this tower actually fails. Measured
  separately (tools/measure_feature_displacement.py), as a fraction of the distance
  a real pose change produces:
        different background ...  1%      shift the image 40 px .... 31%
        A DIFFERENT ROBOT ......  4%      zoom out to 0.75x ........ 41%
                                          a real RoboCasa frame .... 102%
  A fifth camera geometry is out of distribution and the representation breaks. The
  only four the tower ever saw put the gripper in tight 12-22 px clusters.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW SHARPLY IS IT TUNED?  (centred cosine vs the real distance between poses)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  pose separation      contrastive      stock
  0-1 cm  (same pose)    +0.974         +0.108
  1-5 cm                 +0.239         +0.098
  5-10 cm                +0.034         +0.045
  10-20 cm               -0.074         -0.010
  20-100 cm              -0.137         -0.106

  This is worth noticing and is not entirely good news. The falloff is very sharp:
  poses 1-5 cm apart already drop from +0.974 to +0.239. The tower behaves more like
  a lookup that identifies WHICH of the 48 canonical poses it is seeing than like a
  smooth embedding of pose as a continuous quantity.
  For retrieval that is ideal. For a policy it is less so: a controller wants nearby
  poses to have nearby features, so that a small error in the visual estimate makes
  a small error in the action. A representation that is peaked at 48 discrete points
  offers little to interpolate between. Training on many more poses -- the newer
  8000-pair export -- is the obvious thing to check here.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT THE DUAL-TOWER DP EXPERIMENT
================================================================================
  SUPPORTS: the premise. The frozen tower really does encode EEF pose in a way that
  ignores robot identity, on robots it never saw. If a policy can read that, it has
  embodiment-invariant pose information for free.

  DOES NOT ADDRESS: whether a policy CAN read it on real images. Every number above
  is measured on synthetic renders. The same tower places a real RoboCasa frame 102%
  of the way from a synthetic frame to a completely unrelated image -- fully
  decorrelated. That is why a single frozen tower gave the policy nothing
  (CLAUDE.md 9.6: frozen 0.0388 against a stock tower's 0.0387), and it is the
  reason to expect the dual-tower runs to help modestly at best.

  The measurement that will settle it is the aux-dropout run, D_freeze_c_drop. If
  dropping the frozen branch 30% of the time costs nothing, the policy was not
  reading it in the first place.
