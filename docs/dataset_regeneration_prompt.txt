================================================================================

Please re-render the `eef_pairs` synthetic embodiment dataset to a new design. The
current version works for its original purpose but fails, in a specific and measured
way, when what it teaches has to transfer to real robot images. Every request below
carries the measurement that motivated it, so you can judge trade-offs yourself rather
than following instructions blindly. Please tell me what you can and cannot do, and
flag anything that conflicts with how your renderer works, BEFORE you start rendering.

WHAT THE DATA IS FOR
  A model sees two images of a robot arm and must say how the end-effector moved
  between them: Cartesian translation, rotation, and gripper. It trains on many
  synthetic embodiments and must generalise to (a) morphologies it never saw and (b)
  real robot trajectories in real kitchens. (a) already works -- 0.67 cm error on
  held-out embodiments against a 4.66 cm predict-the-mean baseline. (b) is what this
  redesign is for.

================================================================================
THE CORE IDEA: HOLD EVERYTHING FIXED EXCEPT THE ROBOT'S MOTION
================================================================================
Fix the camera, the lighting, and the scene. Then vary ONLY:
    - the end-effector pose (many samples)
    - the gripper state
    - which embodiment is rendered

The visual difference between two frames is then PURELY the robot's motion, with no
nuisance variation to explain away. Three measurements say this is the right trade:

1. The nuisance axes were never doing much work. Perturbing an image and measuring how
   far its features move, as a fraction of the distance a real pose change produces:
       different background ...  1%       shift the image 40 px ....  31%
       furniture recolour .....  2%       zoom out to 0.75x .......  41%
       A DIFFERENT ROBOT ......  4%       a real kitchen frame .... 102%
   Background and furniture cost almost nothing to drop. The 31-41% axis is camera
   geometry, and fixing the camera is what removes it.

2. The current 4 camera views are not interchangeable. Over 600 pose pairs on a fixed
   robot, the SAME 3-D displacement produces pixel displacements whose magnitude varies
   by a median factor of 1.6 across views, up to 7x in individual cases -- a 0.20 m
   motion moves the gripper 6 px in one view and 43 px in another. With a world-frame
   label the model must first work out which view it is in. Four is few enough to
   memorise, and that is measurably what happened.

3. THE BUDGET. Today: 893,270 frames = 111 renders for every (pose, embodiment) pair,
   spent on 12 background/view combinations x 3 colour variants x 2 gripper x 2
   furniture. Under the fixed design only the 2 gripper states remain, so the SAME
   total render count buys 55x more poses: 144 -> about 8,000. Since motion pairs grow
   roughly with the square of the pose count, the motion-vocabulary limit effectively
   disappears. That is by far the best use of the budget: pose diversity is the axis
   the task is actually about.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. CAMERA -- MATCH THE DEPLOYMENT CAMERA EXACTLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use the same camera as the target domain: RoboCasa `robot0_agentview_right`, the
robot-base-mounted view used by the real trajectories (the Jaco / Panda / IIWA / UR5e
renders in visual_robust_new_barx_ur5e). Same pose, same intrinsics, same resolution.

Rationale: this removes the domain gap instead of asking the model to bridge it. The
current 4 views are arbitrary and match nothing, which is the worst of both worlds -- a
head trained on them locates the gripper to 3-5 px on robots it has NEVER seen, and
collapses to near chance on real images from a fifth camera.

  * the camera must be IDENTICAL between the two frames of a pair. If it moves, camera
    motion and robot motion mix in the image and the label stops being recoverable at
    all. This holds regardless of anything else.
  * please still export camera extrinsics and intrinsics as columns. The current export
    has none, which is why the gripper's pixel position could not be recomputed later
    and the whole dataset had to be re-exported just to add it. Exporting them also
    lets us move to a varied-camera design later without re-rendering.
  * ALSO NOTE: today `background_index` is not independent of the camera --
    bg mod 4 == view exactly -- so "12 backgrounds" is really 4 views x 3 backdrops.
    If you keep any background variation at all, make it a genuinely separate axis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. LIGHTING AND SCENE -- ONE OF EACH, AND MAKE IT THE DEPLOYMENT SCENE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One lighting setup, one scene. Going further than "empty room": if you can, make that
one scene resemble the target -- a RoboCasa kitchen with a sink and objects on the
counter, rather than a robot on a checkerboard floor.

Measurement: a real kitchen frame sits 102% of the way from a synthetic frame to a
completely unrelated image -- fully decorrelated. Camera and scene content are the two
axes on which the domains actually differ, and fixing the camera addresses only one.

If matching the kitchen is too costly, one fixed simple scene is still fine and is a
clear improvement on the current design. Say which you chose.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SAMPLE MOTION PAIRS, NOT A POSE GRID  (spend the freed budget here)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The current poses were sampled to COVER the reachable workspace, so pairing them gives
motions unlike any real trajectory segment. Even restricting to pairs closer than
0.15 m, the mean rotation between them is 48 degrees; a real trajectory covering the
same 0.09 m rotates about 6.5.

It shows up directly in the labels. Bucketing motions into "slightly / moderately /
far" by quantile:
    translation   synthetic 0.028 / 0.073 / 0.110 m    real 0.026 / 0.072 / 0.107 m
                  -> agree to 7%. "move forward moderately" means the same thing.
    rotation      synthetic 9.7 / 29.2 / 63.8 deg      real 1.0 / 3.9 / 8.8 deg
                  -> 7x apart. The same words mean different physical rotations.

PLEASE DO
  * for each sample: pick a reachable state s_t, sample a LOCAL delta_eef, compute
    s_t+h, render both frames. Pairs, not a grid.
  * aim for the bulk of the distribution around |translation| ~ 0.10 m and rotation
    ~5-10 degrees, with a tail of larger motions rather than centring on them.
  * store both states AND the delta in the row.
  * CRITICAL -- draw the set of pose pairs ONCE, then render EVERY embodiment at that
    same set. Do not sample fresh motions per robot. This is the single most valuable
    property of the current dataset: because every embodiment is posed identically,
    knowing which robot you are looking at tells you nothing about the label, so the
    model cannot cheat by recognising the robot. It must survive the redesign.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ROTATION -- IT DOES NOT CURRENTLY WORK. FIX IT OR DOCUMENT IT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Frames are 180x320 and the gripper spans about 20 px, so a 5-degree rotation moves the
gripper's extremities by 0.87 px. A vision transformer patch covers 10 px of the
original frame, so a realistic rotation changes the image by roughly ONE ELEVENTH OF
ONE PATCH. It is below the resolution at which the model can see anything.

Measured relative error (prediction error / actual rotation):
    synthetic, ~60 degree rotations ....  24%   learned
    real,      ~6.5 degree rotations ...  95%   not learned -- and in 6 runs out of 6
                                                it was WORSE than predicting no
                                                rotation at all
On top of that, half the real rotation is not signal: over 25 frames the gripper
travels 12.3 degrees of path but ends only 6.5 degrees from where it started (ratio
0.53). Translation's equivalent ratio is 0.88.

PLEASE DO at least one of:
  a) render at higher resolution -- 512x512 native beats upsampling 180x320. This is
     the cheapest real fix and it also helps the gripper-state and fine-position
     signals.
  b) add a wrist camera, where the gripper fills the frame.
  c) if neither is possible, still ship the rotation labels, but WRITE THE LIMITATION
     INTO THE DATASET CARD in plain terms, e.g.:

         "Rotation labels: at 180x320 with the gripper spanning ~20 px, a 5-degree
          rotation displaces the gripper's extremities by <1 px, which is below one
          model patch. Rotation is therefore not reliably learnable from these images
          at realistic magnitudes. Translation and gripper labels are unaffected.
          Use rotation supervision only if rendering at higher resolution."

     Please include this note regardless of which option you pick, adjusted to match
     whatever resolution you end up using -- downstream users need to know.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SCHEMA -- FACTORS IN COLUMNS, NOT IN DIRECTORY NAMES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Today the gripper state is encoded by WHICH SUBSET a frame lives in (`_closed` vs
`_open`), and the furniture variant likewise. Two costs: the gripper can only take 2
values when a real gripper is continuous, and getting any gripper supervision requires
pairing frames ACROSS subsets -- which is easy to get wrong, and did go wrong here
(pairing across `_furniture` too meant the kitchen was being repainted between the two
frames of a single "motion").

ONE dataset, with these columns:
    observation.state            xyz(3) + quaternion_xyzw(4) + gripper(1), CONTINUOUS
    observation.state_next       the same 8 values at s_t+h
    observation.delta_eef        translation(3) + rotation quaternion_xyzw(4)
    observation.embodiment_index
    observation.camera_extrinsics    4x4, or position + quaternion
    observation.camera_intrinsics    fx, fy, cx, cy
    observation.eef_pixel            (u,v) -- for BOTH frames if possible
    observation.eef_in_frame
    (any remaining nuisance axis, e.g. colour variant, as a COLUMN)

Please state the quaternion convention (xyzw or wxyz) in the dataset card. The two
corpora in this project use OPPOSITE conventions and it is documented nowhere, which is
a silent and very hard-to-find source of wrong labels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SHIP AN EMBODIMENT NAME MAPPING  (very cheap, unblocks a key analysis)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`observation.embodiment_index` is an integer with no key anywhere -- not in meta/, not
in the dataset card. So held-out splits can only be random, and "unseen ARM" cannot be
told apart from "unseen GRIPPER", which is one of the questions this project most wants
to answer. Please ship `meta/embodiments.json` mapping each index to
{"arm": ..., "gripper": ...}.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECKS TO RUN BEFORE SHIPPING -- each corresponds to a failure we actually hit
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RECONSTRUCTION. On a sample of rows, assert that applying observation.delta_eef to
   observation.state reproduces observation.state_next: position to 1e-9, rotation to
   1e-6, comparing quaternions sign-invariantly (q and -q are the same rotation).
2. NO SHORTCUT. Group by embodiment and confirm the sorted list of (pose_t, pose_h) is
   IDENTICAL for every robot. If it is not, embodiment identity leaks into the label.
3. MOTION REALISM. Print the mean |translation| and mean rotation magnitude over your
   pairs. Target ~0.10 m and ~5-10 degrees. If rotation comes out near 50 degrees, the
   sampling is still workspace-covering rather than local.
4. CAMERA CONSTANCY. Confirm the camera parameters are byte-identical between the two
   frames of every pair, and across the dataset if you took the fixed-camera design.
5. PIXEL COVERAGE. Histogram observation.eef_pixel. With a fixed camera it should still
   cover the frame broadly, because the POSES vary. If it forms a few tight clusters,
   the pose sampling is too narrow -- that is the current dataset's failure mode.
6. COMPLETE UPLOAD. Confirm `videos/` exists and its file count matches the episode
   count. One subset shipped with data/ and meta/ but no videos/: info.json declared
   231,678 frames and not one of them could be decoded.
7. NO NULLS INSIDE LIST COLUMNS. Rows where the EEF is out of frame must store NaN
   floats, not Arrow nulls. Nulls inside a list column report null_count = 0 and look
   like NaN in pandas, but break the `datasets` library at load time with
   "Could not infer dtype of NoneType". Note that writing NaN through pandas
   `to_parquet` silently converts it BACK to null -- build the array with pyarrow.
