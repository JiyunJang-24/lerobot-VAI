SHORT ANSWERS TO THE THREE QUESTIONS
------------------------------------
1. "SmolVLM gets the current and next image and predicts the motion between them?"
   Yes. Exactly that, with the answer written as an English sentence.

2. "How is the dataset made, especially the labels?"
   Section 2. The label is NOT hand-written: it is derived from the recorded
   end-effector displacement and quantised into direction + magnitude words, with
   the magnitude thresholds fitted as quantiles of the training data.

3. "Does it do well at positions it has seen?"
   NO -- and this is the main finding. It does EQUALLY badly at seen and unseen
   positions, and at seen and unseen embodiments. Section 5 gives the numbers in
   interpretable terms. The honest summary is that it gets the DIRECTION of a
   motion component right about 60% of the time while naming more components than
   actually happened, gets direction AND magnitude right about 37%, and never once
   produced a fully correct sentence out of 400 attempts.


================================================================================
1. THE TASK
================================================================================
    input   [image_t, image_t+h]  +  "How did the gripper move from the first
                                       image to the second?"
    output  "move forward moderately, move right slightly, turn left slightly,
             close gripper"

Model: SmolVLM2-500M-Video-Instruct, all 507M parameters trained (86M vision tower
+ 362M text model + connector). Cross-entropy on the ANSWER tokens only -- the
prompt and the 128 image placeholder tokens are masked out, or the model would
spend capacity re-predicting a fixed prompt.

Cost per sample is 165 tokens: 64 per image after the connector pools each image's
1024 patches, plus the prompt, plus ~10 answer tokens. `do_image_splitting` is
turned OFF; leaving it on tiles each frame into 26 crops and inflates one sample
from 165 tokens to 1755 for no added detail at these resolutions.

Training: 4000 steps, batch 8, lr 1e-5, AdamW, bf16. About 80 minutes per run on
one H100. Eight variants were run in parallel.


================================================================================
2. THE DATASET, AND HOW THE LABEL IS BUILT
================================================================================

2.1 SOURCE
    ChiefJang/visual_robust_barx
      -> eef_pairs/56combo_8000_local_motion_27cam_kitchen_l8s0
    local: /dataset/jiyun/dataset_git/eef_pairs/56combo_8000_local_motion_27cam_kitchen_l8s0
    22 GB, converted to LeRobot v3.0.

    ONE ROW IS ONE TRAINING PAIR. `observation.images.current` and `.next` are two
    separate video streams, so nothing has to be assembled -- this is a large
    improvement on the previous export, where a pair had to be built by finding two
    rows sharing an embodiment, background, camera and colour while differing in
    pose, and every one of those axes was an index that could be silently misaligned.

    448,000 pairs = 56 embodiments x 8000 motions
    27 cameras, one kitchen scene, both 180x320 and 512x910 renders

2.2 FILTERING APPLIED
    448,000  raw
    426,104  gripper visible in BOTH frames (95.1%); a pair with the EEF out of
             frame has no visual evidence for its label, so it is noise rather than
             a hard example
    365,232  after dropping the 8 xarm7_gripper embodiments -- their renders do not
             match their labels (at an identical pose and camera the other grippers
             produce near-identical images while those show the arm in unrelated
             configurations). The metadata gives no hint: same pose set, same 0.951
             visibility. A silent image/label mismatch.
    -> 48 embodiments remain, a clean 8 arms x 6 grippers grid.

2.3 THE LABEL -- THIS IS THE PART THAT MATTERS
    The sentence is generated, never written by hand. Pipeline:

    (a) TAKE THE RECORDED DISPLACEMENT.
        `observation.delta_eef_cam` = the end-effector displacement between the two
        frames, expressed in the CAMERA's frame: translation (3) + rotation
        quaternion xyzw (4). Plus `observation.gripper_delta` in {-1, 0, +1}.

        Camera frame, not world frame, because there are 27 cameras. With a
        world-frame label the model would have to identify which camera it is
        looking through before it could use the image at all -- the same 3-D
        displacement produces very different pixel motion from different viewpoints.
        A camera-frame label co-varies with the camera, so the question stays
        answerable from the images alone.

    (b) CONVERT THE ROTATION TO EULER ANGLES, sign-consistently.
        Quaternions are canonicalised to the w >= 0 hemisphere FIRST. q and -q are
        the same rotation but give euler angles differing by pi, which would put one
        physical motion into opposite word buckets.

    (c) FIT MAGNITUDE THRESHOLDS AS QUANTILES OF THE TRAINING DATA.
        Not fixed constants. Three buckets -- "slightly", "moderately", "far" --
        with cut points at the 35th, 75th and 93rd percentile of |value|, computed
        over the TRAINING rows only.

        Quantiles rather than constants because the same word then covers the same
        share of the distribution regardless of corpus scale, and because a label
        set dominated by one phrase is memorisable. An earlier version of this
        objective reached 1.000 content accuracy by 15k steps and then taught the
        model nothing further.

        Measured on this data: translation idle < 0.028 m, slight < 0.073,
        moderate < 0.110.

    (d) EMIT ONE CLAUSE PER AXIS THAT EXCEEDS THE IDLE THRESHOLD.
        translation  +x/-x -> "move forward/backward"
                     +y/-y -> "move left/right"
                     +z/-z -> "move up/down"
        rotation     roll left/right, tilt up/down, turn left/right
        gripper      "open gripper" / "close gripper"
        nothing above threshold -> "stay still"

        Joined with commas in that fixed order, e.g.
        "move backward moderately, move up slightly, roll right slightly, close gripper"

2.4 WHAT THE LABELS LOOK LIKE
    mean translation magnitude   0.096 m
    mean rotation magnitude      4.4 degrees
    gripper changes in           49% of pairs
    clauses per sentence         4.3 on average

    The translation and rotation magnitudes now match real robot trajectories
    (0.09 m and ~6.5 degrees over a comparable interval). The previous export
    sampled poses to COVER the workspace rather than to resemble trajectory
    segments, and produced 48-degree rotations -- a 7x mismatch that made the same
    word mean different physical things in the two domains. That is fixed.

2.5 SPLITS
    The 48 embodiments form a complete 8 arms x 6 grippers grid, which matters:

      random   holds out 12 COMBINATIONS. Every arm and every gripper is still
               present in training. Verified: zero never-seen arms, zero never-seen
               grippers. This is NOT an unseen-hardware test, and every held-out
               result in this project before now was of this kind.
      arm      holds out every embodiment using 2 of the 8 arms (12 embodiments).
               Those arms are never seen in any form.
      gripper  holds out every embodiment using 2 of the 6 grippers (16).


================================================================================
3. THE EIGHT RUNS
================================================================================
    cam        baseline: camera-frame labels, 180x320, random (combination) holdout
    cam_s1     same, seed 1 -- gives the seed noise floor
    arm        holdout by ARM (never-seen arms)
    gripper    holdout by GRIPPER (never-seen grippers)
    world      world-frame labels instead of camera-frame
    cam_norot  rotation words removed from the label
    high       512x910 renders instead of 180x320
    high_arm   512x910 AND holdout by arm

All share data, schedule, batch and learning rate. Scores are clause-level Jaccard:
"move left slightly" is ONE claim that must match in axis, direction and magnitude.
Claims are parsed by clause, not as a bag of words, because "move left slightly" and
"roll left slightly" share tokens and a bag-of-words score mixes the two categories.

  cam_s1         0.197     0.158    0.183     0.168     28/95       0.198      0.196
  arm            0.169     0.169    0.199     0.162     41/95       0.181      0.172
  gripper        0.202     0.162    0.188     0.168     41/94       0.212      0.209
  world          0.159     0.185    0.246     0.159     52/96       0.116      0.120
  cam_norot      0.216     0.195    0.204       --      14/74       0.217      0.222
  high           0.208     0.203    0.235     0.200     76/93       0.152      0.159
  high_arm       0.211     0.208    0.230     0.212     77/93       0.182      0.169

  "distinct" = how many DIFFERENT sentences the model produced, out of how many
  distinct sentences the ground truth contained.

4.1 RESOLUTION IS THE ONE BIG EFFECT
    Prediction diversity goes from 28-52 of ~95 (30-49%) at 180x320 to 76-77 of 93
    (82%) at 512x910. At low resolution the model partially collapses onto a few
    stock sentences; at high resolution it does not.

    Rotation improves with it, 0.147-0.168 -> 0.200-0.212. That was a prediction:
    at 180x320 the gripper spans about 20 px and a 5-degree rotation moves its
    extremities 0.87 px, roughly one ninth of a vision-transformer patch. It was
    below the resolution at which anything could be seen.

4.2 THE REAL-DATA COLUMN IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY
    Those numbers evaluate on real Jaco trajectories, and they are contaminated by a
    convention mismatch that is my error: the models are trained on camera-frame (or
    world-frame) labels, while the real-data evaluation builds its labels in the
    END-EFFECTOR frame -- a convention none of the models ever saw. The real corpus
    has no camera extrinsics, which is why that choice was made originally.
    Comparisons WITHIN the camera-frame runs are still meaningful; the absolute
    values, and any cam-versus-world comparison on real data, are not.


================================================================================
5. THE MAIN FINDING: IT IS NOT GOOD ANYWHERE, INCLUDING WHERE IT HAS MOST DATA
================================================================================

5.1 WHY THIS WAS CHECKED
    The gripper's pixel position is concentrated: across all 448,000 pairs it spans
    u 242-637 of 910, mean 410 with std 55, so the left 27% and right 30% of the
    frame are never visited. Vertically the coverage is full. So the natural worry
    was that the held-out score is dragged down by thin coverage -- and the natural
    test is to score both splits where coverage is DENSEST.

5.2 SEEN VS HELD-OUT, BINNED BY POSITION
    Equal-count bins of |u - mode|, with bin edges taken from the held-out split so
    both splits are compared on the same position distribution. The core bin is
    30 px wide (u 395-424).

      model       CORE seen  CORE held    gap   |  all bins seen  all bins held
      -------------------------------------------------------------------------
      cam            0.176      0.168   +0.009  |      0.166          0.157
      cam_s1         0.172      0.153   +0.019  |      0.164          0.153
      arm            0.164      0.158   +0.006  |      0.162          0.158
      gripper        0.172      0.141   +0.031  |      0.168          0.148
      high           0.192      0.175   +0.017  |      0.200          0.179
      high_arm       0.195      0.178   +0.017  |      0.200          0.171

    MORPHOLOGY IS NEARLY FREE. seen minus held-out is +0.006 to +0.031, and the
    seed spread between cam and cam_s1 is 0.010, so most of those gaps sit at or
    barely above noise. The `arm` split -- embodiments whose ARM was never seen in
    any combination -- has the SMALLEST gap of the six, +0.006.

    POSITION IS NOT THE LIMIT EITHER. Core and all-bin numbers agree to within
    0.01-0.03, and a per-bin sweep across the visited range is flat.

    CAVEAT ON SCOPE: this can only test positions the data visits. The never-visited
    outer 57% of the frame cannot be evaluated, because there is no data there.

5.3 WHAT 0.19 ACTUALLY MEANS
    Jaccard is abstract, so the best model (`high`) was scored again in
    interpretable terms, 200 samples per split:

                        SEEN      HELD-OUT
      exact sentence    0.000       0.000     never fully correct, 0 of 400
      direction recall  0.600       0.567     names the right (axis, direction)
      direction+size    0.373       0.339     ...and the right magnitude word
      clauses produced  5.7         5.9       against 4.3 in the ground truth
      distinct outputs  134/187     135/184

    Read carefully: the 60% direction figure is inflated by over-production. The
    model emits 5.7-5.9 clauses where the truth has 4.3, so it names motion
    components that did not happen, and naming more raises recall for free. Jaccard
    charges for that, which is why it reads 0.19 rather than 0.60.

    A typical held-out example:
      pred: "move forward slightly, move left slightly, move up slightly,
             roll left slightly, tilt up slightly"
      true: "move forward slightly, move up far, turn left slightly, close gripper"
    Right about "forward slightly", right that it moved up but wrong that it was
    slight, invents "left" and "roll left", and misses the gripper closing.

5.4 SO THE ANSWER TO "DOES IT DO WELL WHERE IT HAS SEEN?"
    No. It reaches 0.195 in the densest 30 pixels of the frame on embodiments it
    trained on -- essentially the same as everywhere else and essentially the same
    as on embodiments it has never seen. Both splits plateau together at a low
    level.

    That is informative rather than merely disappointing: the bottleneck is NOT
    morphology generalisation and NOT data coverage. Adding embodiments, cameras or
    renders should not be expected to move it. The same conclusion is visible in
    section 4.1, where raising the resolution took prediction diversity from 30-49%
    to 82% while the score moved only 0.15 -> 0.20: the model learned to answer more
    diversely, not more correctly.


================================================================================
5.5 IS THE MOTION BIG ENOUGH TO SEE? YES. THE LABEL IS THE PROBLEM.
================================================================================
    Displacement magnitudes, and what they are worth in pixels:

      |d| < 5 cm    12% of pairs     26 px at 910 width   ( 9 px at 320)
      5-10 cm       48%              53 px                (19 px)
      10-15 cm      30%              84 px                (29 px)
      > 15 cm       11%             125 px                (44 px)
      median 0.090 m                 60 px                (21 px)

    The gripper itself spans roughly 20 px at 320 width, so the MEDIAN motion is
    about one gripper width. That is plainly visible. Only 12% of pairs are small
    enough to be genuinely hard.

    But the label does not ask "did it move and which way". It asks for a THREE-AXIS
    decomposition, each with a three-way magnitude. Breaking the model's answers
    down that way (400 seen samples, `high`, translation only):

      axis                 truth mentions  direction   dir+size   names it when
                                              right                truth does NOT
      x forward/backward         65%          0.800      0.485         0.786
      y left/right               63%          0.632      0.411         0.864
      z up/down                  65%          0.571      0.352         0.906

      truth magnitude        n    direction right   direction AND magnitude
      slightly             492         0.650                 0.636
      moderately           200         0.655                 0.045
      far                   82         0.805                 0.000

    TWO BEHAVIOURS EXPLAIN EVERYTHING ABOVE.

    (1) It names essentially every axis every time. When the truth says an axis did
        not move, the model claims it did 79-91% of the time. So the 0.600 direction
        recall is coverage, not discrimination. Only x is read meaningfully at
        0.800; z sits at 0.571, close to the 0.5 floor for guessing a sign on an
        axis you always mention -- and depth is exactly the axis one camera sees
        worst.

    (2) The magnitude word is a constant. Given a correct direction, magnitude is
        right 98% of the time when the answer is "slightly", 7% when "moderately",
        and 0% when "far" -- it never emits that word at all. Magnitude is not
        partially learned; it is not learned.

    So the intuition that only DIRECTION, and only for large motions, is solidly
    judgeable from two frames is supported -- with the caveat that even direction is
    solid only on the axis most visible to the camera.

================================================================================
6. WHAT IS LEFT, IN ORDER OF WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS
================================================================================
  1. FIX THE OVER-PRODUCTION FIRST -- it is the largest and cheapest win. The model
     asserts motion on an axis that did not move 79-91% of the time (5.5). Predict
     each axis as an explicit 3-way class (negative / none / positive) instead of
     letting the model choose which clauses to emit. That removes the free-recall
     loophole and makes the score mean what it appears to mean.
  2. DROP OR SIMPLIFY THE MAGNITUDE WORD. It is currently a constant "slightly"
     (5.5): 0.045 correct on "moderately", 0.000 on "far". Either regress the
     magnitude as a number, or reduce it to two buckets, or remove it and measure
     direction alone -- which is what the two-frame evidence actually supports.
  3. CAPACITY AND SCHEDULE. 500M parameters and 4000 steps at batch 8. The high-res
     runs had not plateaued.
  4. FIX THE REAL-DATA EVALUATION. It currently scores against a label convention
     the models were never trained on (4.2). Either express the real labels in the
     camera frame -- which needs camera extrinsics the real export does not have --
     or retrain on end-effector-frame labels for that comparison.
  5. TRANSLATION AUGMENTATION. Random shift/crop would move the gripper across the
     full frame width for free, covering the outer 57% that no render visits. Note
     5.2 found no degradation inside the visited range, so this is about robustness
     for the policy corpus rather than about the scores in this report.
